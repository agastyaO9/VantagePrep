The Problem

Little Classes. Big Progress.

Most tutoring programs pack in as many students as possible. Parents pay, drop their kids off, and hope for the best, with no real way to know what's actually happening in the room.

The "Black Box" Problem

You send your child to class and trust that learning happens. But you have no visibility into how they're doing, what they understand, or what they're struggling with.

Lost in the Crowd

In a room of 20–30 kids, quiet struggles go unnoticed. Students fall behind silently and often finish a course having learned almost nothing.

No Real Accountability

Without regular testing or pressure to perform, there's no way to know if a student actually absorbed the material, or just sat through it.

This is uncommon, and that's the point

Our Approach

Ten students, max. One instructor. Total transparency. No exceptions.

This is the core of everything Vantage Prep does. While most programs maximize headcount, Vantage Prep intentionally caps every class at 10 students so that every single child gets real, individual, working attention, not a lecture they sit through and forget.

Program 01

SAT Introduction to Algebra

A focused, two-week program for Grades 6–8, and Vantage Prep's very first cohort. Here's the format, the schedule, and what your child needs to start.

2 weeks Mon / Wed / Fri class Sat office hours Friday test day Capped at 10 students $30
This class is not for everyone, and that's intentional. Unlike programs that accept anyone at any level, Vantage Prep requires students to have basic pre-algebra skills before joining, so class time is spent building real math fluency, not re-teaching arithmetic. This keeps every session focused and effective for the whole group.

Here's a practice test from Weber State University to help you check your child's pre-algebra readiness before enrolling.

Open practice placement exam (PDF)

What Your Child Needs to Start

  • Comfort with basic pre-algebra (order of operations, simple equations, negative numbers)
  • Ability to read and follow multi-step math problems
  • Willingness to be tested and corrected. This is an active class, not a passive one

What Your Child Will Master

  • Solving linear equations and inequalities, from one-step problems to multi-step, real-world constraints
  • Graphing lines and reading their meaning: slope, intercepts, and y = mx + b as real-world rates of change
  • Solving systems of two linear equations (substitution, elimination, and graphing) and applying it all to word problems

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions from Parents

How much does the program cost?
The full two-week program is $30 per student, including all class sessions, office hours, resources, and practice tests.
What happens if my child doesn't score above 70%?
You receive a full refund, no questions asked. I stand behind the results of my own teaching.
Does my child need prior experience?
Yes. Students should be comfortable with basic pre-algebra concepts. This ensures class time is spent building real skills rather than reviewing fundamentals.
Can I sit in on a class?
Absolutely. Parents are welcome and encouraged to join any Zoom session to see exactly how the class runs.
How big are the classes really?
Every class is capped at a strict maximum of 10 students, so your child always gets individual attention.
What is the final assessment?
It is a full-length practice test modeled directly on the math sections of the SAT. Everything your child works on during the two weeks builds toward it, and it is the same assessment the 70% money-back guarantee is measured against.
How do I pay, and when?
Payment is handled securely through the Stripe link built into the sign-up form. You'll pay the $30 as the last step of signing up, and your child's spot is confirmed once payment goes through.
What if my child misses a session?
They can catch up during the Saturday office hours block, and we'll send along anything they missed by email so they're not behind for the next class.
What materials will my child need?
Just a pencil, a notebook or graph paper, and a device for Zoom. A basic or scientific calculator is allowed but not required.
How do I know my child is actually improving?
Every Friday is a real test day, and you'll receive their score and progress notes directly by email each week.
